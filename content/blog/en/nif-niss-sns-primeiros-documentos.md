Anyone arriving in Portugal to work and build a life hears three acronyms in the first few days: **NIF**, **NISS** and **SNS**. They are the foundation of almost everything — working, opening a bank account, paying contributions, seeing a doctor. This guide explains what each one is and how to obtain them without unnecessary detours.

## NIF: your tax number

The **Tax Identification Number (NIF)** is issued by the Tax Authority and lets you work, open a bank account, rent a home and meet your tax obligations. In practice, it is the first document to sort out.

- **Resident in Portugal:** requested with your home-country civil ID or passport and your residence permit.
- **Not yet resident:** you can still obtain a NIF, but you need to appoint a **tax representative** resident in Portugal.

## NISS: your Social Security number

The **Social Security Identification Number (NISS)** gives access to social rights and support and is used to meet your contribution duties. It can be requested in two ways:

1. By yourself, through the form on the Social Security portal (the "Sou Cidadão" menu);
2. By your **employer**, at the point of hiring.

When applying, a foreign citizen generally presents three types of document: identification, proof of employment situation, and proof of the residence permit.

## SNS: your health service user number

The **National Health Service (SNS)** user number lets you register at a health centre, have a family doctor and access public healthcare. It matters especially for families with children.

## Can it all be done at once?

Yes. Foreign nationals with an address in Portugal can request the **NIF, NISS and SNS user number** in a single step. Bringing the right documents from the start avoids repeat visits and speeds up the whole integration process.

> Order matters: no NIF means no work and no bank account; no NISS means your contributions are not properly recorded. Sorting all three early saves weeks of bureaucracy.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Rental or employment contracts signed **before** obtaining a NIF;
- Identification documents **past their validity** at the time of the request;
- Delegating the request to someone else without proper **authorisation** or power of attorney.
